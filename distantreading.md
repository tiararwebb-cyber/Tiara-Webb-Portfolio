Question: Is Zulu the first Bantu language
Chat's response: No. Zulu is not the first Bantu language.

Linguists believe that all Bantu languages, including Zulu, descended from a much older ancestral language called Proto-Bantu, which was spoken thousands of years ago in West-Central Africa before Bantu-speaking peoples migrated across much of sub-Saharan Africa. Zulu is one of the many modern languages that developed from that earlier language.

Where Zulu fits in the Bantu family

A simplified family tree looks like this:

Proto-Bantu
    ↓
Bantu Languages
    ↓
Southern Bantu
    ↓
Nguni Languages
    ↓
Zulu (isiZulu)


The Zulu language is therefore a descendant of earlier Bantu languages rather than the original or first Bantu language. The article classifies Zulu within the Bantu family and specifically the Nguni branch.

Is Zulu one of the oldest Bantu languages?

Not necessarily. Most modern Bantu languages evolved over long periods from earlier forms, and it is generally not possible to identify one living Bantu language as "the first." Languages such as Zulu, Xhosa, Swahili, and Northern Ndebele are all branches of the larger Bantu family that developed over time.

Why people sometimes think Zulu is the first

Because Zulu is one of the best-known and most widely spoken Bantu languages, some people mistakenly assume it came first. In fact, the article notes that it is among the most widely spoken Bantu languages and is the most widely spoken home language in South Africa.

So the short answer is:

No, Zulu is not the first Bantu language. It is a modern Bantu language that evolved from the ancient Proto-Bantu language and belongs to the Nguni branch of the Bantu family.
